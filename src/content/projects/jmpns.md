## Overview

### What was the problem?

James Marshall Parent Nursery School (JMPNS) had a website that wasn't serving their audience. It was visually cluttered, the information was buried, and the mobile experience wasn't that great. It needed a new coat of paint, but the underlying structure needed to make sense too.

#### Before & After

![Snippets of the JMPNS site before the redesign](/images/jmpns/before-snippets.png)

## My Role

I ran this project from start to finish as the UX designer: discovery, architecture, design, and delivery.

## Approach

Overall, the question I was asking myself was "How can I make this more user-friendly and visually-appealing?". At the time, I wasn't thinking too much about accessibility, but was focused more on the UX methods and figuring out how to organize this content so that a general audience can find what they need.

## Process

### What methods of UX research did I use to help me solve this problem?

#### METHOD 01: DISCOVERY

No judgement, just taking a look-see.
One of the more important steps in UX research is taking stock of what is already there with no judgement (also known as a content or interface inventory, depending on the project). When you start taking a look at each page, button, heading style as part of a whole we can really get to know the content intimately. In reviewing the JMPNS content, I saw that important forms where buried 2 or 3 levels deep, clicking on "About us" didn't lead to the expected page, text was overlapping and hard to read, and other stuff. I got to know the content pretty well and was starting to get a pretty good idea of how to organize it.
![Content inventory](/images/jmpns/ia.png)
![Coggle map](/images/jmpns/coggle.png)
Another part of the discovery process was seeing what other educational agencies where up to. I conducted audits for both "competitors" and "aspirational" schools. Not only did I get an idea of how these other organizations where configuring their content, I got visual inspiration as well.
I conducted user personas, card sorting exercises, user flows all to be able to see what a journey would be like for a potential parent, community member, or educational agency authority. The following are snippets of aspirational websites, chosen for their function, their information architecture, and how they conveyed their brand identity through visual design.
![Screenshots of The Language Academy school website, Woodland Parent Nursery School, and Sacramento Montessori School websites](/images/jmpns/inspo-snippets.png)

#### METHOD 02: EXPLORATION

From the discovery insights, I built user personas and and scenario maps to ground the layout decisions in real behavior. I tested low-fidelity wireframes before I developed the high-fidelity mockups, which meant stakeholder feedback shaped the structure.
![User personas detailed the reasons why they might want to visit the JMPNS website](/images/jmpns/persona-sample.png)
![Scenarios for each user persona](/images/jmpns/scenarios.png)

## Outcome

The final deliverable was a redesigned Wix site with improved navigation, clear information hierarchy, and a mobile-responsible layout that worked on devices that the audience actually used, their phones.

## Skill Stack

- UX research
- Information architecture
- Wireframing
- Wix

## What I'd Do Differently

My accessibility standards have continued to develop since this project. Today, I would integrate contrast checking and accessibility throughout the design process. I would also be more rigorous with spacing and content density decisions earlier in the wireframing.
